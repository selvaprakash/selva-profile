AI Radiology Reporting

For a radiologist in Australia · 2026–present · Architect & lead developer

Radiologists dictate reports that usually go to offshore transcriptionists and come back 4–5 hours later. This app turns dictation into a finished draft in the practice's own templates.

Dictation→ElevenLabs Scribe v2→GPT-5.5 cleanup→Template match→Radiologist review
  • Rescued and rebuiltTook over after a previous developer left before go-live. Rebuilt the product from scratch and reached the earlier feature set in 2 weeks, against a 2-month estimate.
  • Template-driven formattingThe LLM layer strips speech artefacts and fits each dictation to the practice's templates (shoulder X-ray, chest X-ray, ultrasound and more), drawing on reference reports when needed. Live transcription supports quick corrections.
  • Privacy by deletionZero-retention LLM processing, no patient identifiers in transcription, IP-restricted Cloudflare R2 storage, and all recordings and files deleted once a report is finalised. Migration to Australian hosting is underway with the practice's IT team.
Impact

Turnaround cut from 4–5 hours to under a minute. Live since September 2026, with about 2,100 reports in October.

How I build

Architecture principles

Right-size the model

A small model with structured inputs often beats a large one on cost and speed. I start small and add capability only where the output needs it.

Keep providers swappable

Search APIs and models change fast. Each external service sits behind its own layer so it can be replaced without touching the workflow.

Fit the real workflow

Every system follows how the experts already work, with review points where a human signs off, so adoption doesn't depend on retraining people.

Delete what you don't need

Sensitive data is stored only as long as the task requires, behind restricted access, and processed by providers that don't retain it.
